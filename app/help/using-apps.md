## What an app is

An app is a small web app that a member of the Portal wrote and published: a
board, a report, a form, a helper for one team's routine. It is not part of
the Portal. It runs on its own address and always opens in a new browser tab.

## Finding one

Open **Apps** in the left rail. **Available to me** lists the apps other
members opened to you, the ones you used last first. **My apps** lists your
own. One search box looks through names, descriptions and owners.

Click a card to open the app. The link button on the card copies the app's
address, which you can send to a colleague: it works for everyone the app is
open to, and shows a "no access" page that names the owner to everyone else.

The apps address on its own, without the name of an app behind it, is an app
center: every app you may open, your own included, the ones you used last
first, with a search box. Bookmark it when apps are all you came for; the
pages an app address shows when something is wrong ("no access", "not
found") lead back to it through **All apps**.

## Signing in

There is nothing to sign in to. The app's address checks with the Portal who
you are and whether the app is open to you, and lets you in. The first time
this takes a moment and may pass through your company sign-in; after that
you stay signed in to all apps for a week.

An app that nobody used for a while is stopped to save resources. Opening it
starts it again: you see a "starting" page for a few seconds, then the app.

## Who can open an app

The owner chooses one of four audiences:

| Audience | Who gets in |
|---|---|
| Only the owner | nobody else |
| The owner's group | every member of the owner's group |
| Every member | everyone who can sign in to the Portal |
| Anyone | no sign-in at all; offered only where an administrator switched it on, and each app is approved by an administrator first |

## What the app knows about you, and what it uses

- The app is told your name, your username and your group, so it can greet
  you or keep your things apart from other people's. It is not told anything
  else, and it never gets your connectors or your credentials.
- An app that answers with a language model, or reads Jira, GitHub and the
  like, does so with **its owner's** model provider and connectors, not with
  yours. What it shows you is what its owner's accounts can see.
- An app is a colleague's code, not the platform's. Its card names the owner:
  that is who to ask when it misbehaves. What you type into an app goes to
  that app.

## When an app does not open

| The page says | What it means |
|---|---|
| Starting | it was stopped while idle; wait a few seconds |
| No access | the app is not open to you; ask the owner named on the page |
| Taken down | an administrator switched it off, with the reason shown |
| Stopped or crashed | the owner has to look at it; tell them |
| Not found | the address is wrong, or the app was deleted |

A page that has been open for a long time may stop answering because your
session ended. Reload it.

## Making your own

**Templates** lists apps their owners offer as a starting point. Pick one,
give it a name, and you have an app of your own made from that version. To
publish code of your own, see [Building apps](#/help/building-apps).
